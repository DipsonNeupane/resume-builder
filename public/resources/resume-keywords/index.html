Use the language of the work, not a keyword dump

Resume keywords: what actually belongs in your resume

A keyword belongs when it accurately names something you can demonstrate and helps a reader understand your fit.

On this page

  2. The evidence test
  3. Put each term where it earns credibility
  4. Translate accurately, not mechanically
  5. Keyword practices to avoid

Short answer

Start with evidence, not a score.

Use specific job titles, tools, methods, certifications, domain terms and skills when they truthfully describe your experience. Put them beside evidence—in a bullet, project, education entry or accurate skills section. Do not add every term in a posting, hide keywords or treat a target percentage as proof of qualification.

Five useful kinds of resume keyword

  • Role and function: customer success, payroll, laboratory technician, curriculum design
  • Tools and systems: Excel, Salesforce, AutoCAD, Kubernetes
  • Methods and processes: root-cause analysis, month-end close, usability testing
  • Domain knowledge: food safety, public procurement, SaaS onboarding
  • Verified qualifications: degrees, licences, certifications and language proficiency

Action verbs can improve writing, but a generic verb such as “managed” does not replace the specific object, scope and evidence of the work. The job requirement extractor can separate stated requirements from other signals without turning every repeated word into a mandate.

The evidence test

Before adding a term, answer three questions:

  1. Does this phrase accurately describe something I did, learned or earned?
  2. Can I point to where the resume demonstrates it?
  3. Could I discuss it comfortably in an interview?

If the answer is no, leave it out. If the experience is real but the term is new, learn what the employer means before deciding that the wording is equivalent.

Put each term where it earns credibility

WeakSkills: stakeholder management, process improvement, Salesforce

StrongerMapped the support handoff with sales and operations, then rebuilt the Salesforce intake fields to reduce incomplete requests.

The stronger line connects the terms to a real action and context. A skills section can still help with quick scanning, but it should not be the only evidence for a central capability.

Translate accurately, not mechanically

A posting may say “client relationship management” while your resume says “supported member accounts.” If the work genuinely involved ongoing relationships, the employer’s language may make the connection clearer. If you only answered one-time questions, the broader phrase may overstate the work.

Use both a full name and common abbreviation once when it aids understanding—for example, “Search Engine Optimization (SEO)”—but do not create awkward repetitions throughout the document.

Keyword practices to avoid

  • A visible “keywords” paragraph with no context
  • White text, tiny type or off-page terms
  • Repeating a phrase until the resume sounds unnatural
  • Copying soft-skill adjectives without examples
  • Listing exposure to a tool as proficiency
  • Using a keyword score as a prediction of interviews
A better success test

A hiring reader should be able to find the relevant term, understand what you did with it and see why it matters—without needing the job description open beside your resume.
