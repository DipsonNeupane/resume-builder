Worldwide scope without universal rules

Resume or CV by country: a safer way to check local expectations

Terms, personal details, length and document conventions differ. Verify the exact market and application instead of copying a global checklist.

On this page
  1. Use a reliable order of evidence
  2. Starting points from official services
  3. Check differences one by one
  4. Minimise personal information
  5. What this guide does not claim

Short answer

Verify the market and the exact application.

Start with the employer’s instructions, then consult a current government or official employment source for the country where you are applying. Check terminology, personal-information norms, required credentials and file expectations separately. A national guide is context—not permission to ignore the posting, regulated-occupation rules or privacy concerns.

1. Use a reliable order of evidence

  1. The vacancy and application portal
  2. The employer’s official careers guidance
  3. Government employment or recognised public career services
  4. Regulator instructions for licensed occupations
  5. General articles only when stronger sources are silent

This order prevents a broad internet convention from overriding a specific requirement.

2. Starting points from official services

These sources were checked on 30 September 2026. They are examples, not a complete world directory.

  • England: National Careers Service CV guidance
  • European Union: Europass CV guidance
  • Canada: Job Bank resume guidance
  • Australia: Workforce Australia application guidance
  • New Zealand: government careers guidance

3. Check differences one by one

  • Whether the document is normally called a resume or CV in this context
  • Whether photos or personal details are expected, optional or discouraged
  • Typical page length and paper size
  • How education, licences and language proficiency should be expressed
  • Whether references belong in the document
  • Whether the employer requests PDF, Word or an application form

4. Minimise personal information

Do not add a date of birth, government identifier, marital status, banking data or a third party’s contact details merely because an unsourced example contains them. Include only what the application legitimately requires, and confirm how the employer asks to receive sensitive documentation.

5. What this guide does not claim

No country is one hiring market

Practices vary by occupation, sector, employer and time. This page does not claim complete coverage, translated interface support or local legal advice. Recheck the linked source and its update date before relying on it.

ResumeStride supports Unicode content, right-to-left resume direction, A4 and Letter output, and flexible section headings. Those technical capabilities do not automatically localise the English interface or validate a country-specific convention.
