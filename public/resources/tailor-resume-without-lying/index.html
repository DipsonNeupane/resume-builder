How to tailor a resume without making things up

Honest tailoring changes what you emphasize. Fabrication changes what happened.

On this page

  2. Use an evidence ladder
  3. Before and after: accurate translation
  4. What to do when you do not know the number
  5. Review AI suggestions like an editor
  6. Let a real gap remain a gap

Short answer

Start from facts you can verify, then select the most relevant examples, translate unfamiliar wording carefully and add missing context you genuinely remember. Do not invent employers, titles, tools, results, dates, credentials or responsibility. When evidence is adjacent rather than exact, describe the connection instead of erasing the gap.

The line between tailoring and fabrication

Honest tailoring
  • Moving a relevant project higher
  • Naming a tool you actually used
  • Clarifying who you supported
  • Removing less relevant detail
  • Using an accurate industry synonym
Fabrication
  • Changing your title to a role you did not hold
  • Adding a tool you only saw in the posting
  • Inventing a metric or customer count
  • Extending dates to hide a gap
  • Claiming an unearned credential

Use an evidence ladder

Describe each item at the highest level you can honestly support:

  1. Owned: you were accountable for the work or result.
  2. Performed: you completed the work yourself.
  3. Contributed: you had a defined part in a shared effort.
  4. Observed or learned: you received training or exposure but did not perform it independently.
  5. Not experienced: leave it out or name it as a development goal outside the resume.

Problems begin when “observed” becomes “performed,” or “contributed” becomes “led.” Precise verbs protect both credibility and confidence.

Before and after: accurate translation

PostingLead cross-functional incident reviews and drive corrective actions.

Real experienceYou gathered customer reports, reproduced issues and joined reviews run by engineering.

Honest versionInvestigated customer-reported issues, documented reproduction steps, and contributed customer context to engineering incident reviews.

Dishonest versionLed cross-functional incident response and owned corrective-action delivery.

What to do when you do not know the number

Specific results are useful only when they are real. If you cannot verify a percentage, revenue figure or volume:

  • Use a scope you do know: region, process, audience or frequency.
  • Describe the observable outcome without manufacturing precision.
  • Check reliable records if you still have access and are allowed to use them.
  • Leave the number out when uncertainty remains.

“Reduced recurring setup errors by standardizing the checklist” is more credible than an invented “reduced errors by 47%.” The free bullet checker will ask for missing evidence rather than manufacture it.

Review AI suggestions like an editor

An AI-generated sentence is not evidence. For every proposed change, ask:

  • Which original fact supports this claim?
  • Did the verb increase my responsibility?
  • Did it add a result, number, tool or audience?
  • Did it remove context that changes the meaning?
  • Would I say this aloud in an interview?
Your decision stays final

Accept only the parts that preserve your meaning. Reject or rewrite anything that sounds impressive because it moved beyond the source evidence.

Let a real gap remain a gap

If a required skill is absent, you still have choices: apply with adjacent evidence, explain active learning, seek a more suitable role or build the skill through real work. The one choice that does not help is inserting the phrase and hoping no one asks. A private resume vs. job evidence check can help you keep “not demonstrated” separate from “confirmed incompatible.”
