Use occupation data as a question set

Occupation sources can show common tasks, knowledge and work context. Use them to ask what you have actually done and what the vacancy means. Do not paste generic duties into a resume, treat a statistical profile as one employer’s requirements, or turn an occupation label into proof of qualification.

Start worldwide; verify locally

The International Labour Organization’s ISCO-08 organizes jobs by their tasks and duties for international reporting. National systems add local detail. APWICO can use that shared frame to avoid a technology-only view, but any licence, education, wage or outlook fact must remain attached to its jurisdiction and source date.

The first research set is broad—not a private-demand ranking

Production query, saved-job category and Search Console occupation data are unavailable. The initial portfolio therefore uses current official external evidence and deliberately samples nine evidence shapes: technical, healthcare, operations, education, service, skilled trades, research, creative and leadership. It does not claim to rank APWICO user demand.

  • Software developer
  • Registered nurse
  • Project management specialist
  • Primary school teacher
  • Customer service representative
  • Electrician
  • Social science research assistant
  • Graphic designer
  • General and operations manager

What a useful occupation page must add

  • A dated, jurisdiction-labelled source record
  • Central tasks translated into questions, not copied resume claims
  • Examples of direct, adjacent and not-demonstrated evidence
  • Regulated or employer-specific requirements kept distinct
  • A route into the real job-description evidence check
  • Named editorial evidence review and next-review date

Why this portfolio stays small

Reviewed depth before scale

Nine initial pages are published because each has a current official source, occupation-specific examples and an explicit jurisdiction boundary. More titles stay withheld until evidence and review support useful pages; this is not a program for hundreds of generated title variants.
