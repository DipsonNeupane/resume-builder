# ResumeStride pricing, template and extension monetization research

Research date: September 28, 2026  
Scope: product strategy and implementation planning only. No code, Stripe catalog,
entitlement, template, extension-submission or production change is authorized by this document.

## Executive decision

Adopt a three-step value ladder, but do not launch the one-time product until it has a
real product to sell:

1. **Free — build and evaluate.** Keep the genuinely useful resume builder, all seven
   currently-free templates, the present three-document-download allowance per fixed
   30-day period, bounded Jobs/Match, three saved jobs, and free extension capture.
2. **Resume Upgrade — finish and keep (recommended target: US$4.99 one time, not
   US$2.99).** Launch only after at least three genuinely distinct new premium layouts
   exist. It permanently unlocks the account's premium resume layouts and removes the
   ordinary PDF/DOCX allowance for user-owned resume documents, subject only to a
   non-marketing abuse/safety ceiling. It does not unlock deeper Match, expanded Jobs,
   new job-specific versions or AI tailoring.
3. **Pro — active job search (keep US$19.99 / 30 days).** Sell the decision-and-tailoring
   workflow: expanded Jobs and saved-job capacity, full evidence Match, creation of
   separate job-specific versions, reviewed AI suggestions, all layouts, and exports.
   Keep the one-time pass as the default and recurring renewal explicitly optional.

This is **Model D: intent-based ladder**. It recognizes Segment A without weakening the
reason Segment B buys Pro. Segment C is not a separate plan: it enters through the free
extension and maps to Free or Pro based on what the user does after capture.

The immediate recommendation is therefore **no pricing change yet**. First validate the
Segment A offer and build the premium-template inventory. The current seven templates
are already promised as free, so moving two behind a paywall would be value removal,
not a new product.

### One-time and per-document market patterns

One-time offers do exist, but the durable and comprehensible examples are usually
US$4.99 or higher, or a much more expensive lifetime purchase:

| **Guul 1 Resume — US$4.99** | One resume, unlimited PDF/DOCX downloads for 30 days, no renewal | The object and duration are explicit; US$4.99 supports clearer economics than US$2.99 |
| **SkillSheet — US$4.99 per resume** | All templates, limited AI, hosted public link, unlimited PDF/DOCX for that resume | Per-resume ownership is understandable but repeat purchases can feel transactional if job-specific versions multiply |

Sources: [Guul one-time resume](https://guul.io/one-time-payment-resume-builder),
[SkillSheet pricing](https://www.skillsheet.co/pricing), and [Rezi pricing](https://www.rezi.ai/pricing).
The two US$4.99 examples are smaller products than the primary comparison set; they
demonstrate offer mechanics, not validated demand at ResumeStride's scale.

### What the market says, without copying it

1. **Free extensions are normal acquisition surfaces.** Teal, Huntr, Simplify,
   Careerflow and Rezi all let the extension or its basic capture/autofill utility work
   on Free. The monetized event is what comes after capture: depth, tailoring, AI,
   capacity or application workflow.
2. **US$19.99/30 days is not expensive for an active-search suite.** The closest workflow
   subscriptions publicly ask roughly US$29–US$50 monthly, with weekly offers that can
   cost more. ResumeStride should not lower Pro merely because resume-only users reject it.
3. **Useful free resume creation is credible.** Kickresume, Teal, Huntr, Rezi, Jobscan
   and Canva all expose meaningful free building or export value. Free does not need to
   be crippled to convert active searchers.
4. **Template gating works best for template-led products.** ResumeStride's defensible
   value is evidence and decision workflow. Templates can monetize Segment A, but should
   remain a secondary SKU rather than the homepage's dominant story.
5. **One-time products need a named object or durable right.** “Pay to download” is less
   comprehensible than “this account permanently has Resume Upgrade.” A US$2.99 charge
   every time, per file, or per job version would create support and trust problems.

## 2. ResumeStride's as-built economics and entitlement baseline

Repository evidence, not a proposed promise:

- All seven current templates (`Modern`, `Classic`, `Minimal`, `Compact`, `Bold`,
  `Executive`, `Ledger`) are available to every account.
- Free accounts receive three successful server-generated PDF/DOCX downloads per fixed
  30-day period from signup. PDF and DOCX share the allowance.
- Free Jobs currently returns up to 5 results and allows one provider refresh per 24
  hours; Pro returns up to 20 and has no once-daily restriction.
- Free accounts can save 3 jobs. Pro has a 10,000-record safety ceiling, which is an
  abuse/storage bound rather than a marketable allowance.
- Free receives a bounded Match preview; Pro receives the full requirement/evidence
  analysis.
- Creating a job-specific resume requires active Pro. Existing versions can be listed,
  opened, manually updated and reset after Pro expiry; new AI suggestions still require
  active Pro. This is already a good non-hostile retention boundary.
- Extension capture itself has no entitlement and does not call Techmap or OpenAI. Saving
  the capture uses the normal Free/Pro saved-job limits and account workflow.
- OpenAI tailoring is pinned to `gpt-4.1-mini-2025-04-14`. Current official pricing is
  US$0.40 / 1M input tokens and US$1.60 / 1M output tokens. The repository's offline
  planning benchmark estimates about US$0.0011 light, US$0.0024 typical and US$0.0049
  heavy per tailoring request. These are estimates, not production measurements.
- The AI ledger has a global US$25 UTC-month hard cap and a 20-reservations/account/hour
  abuse bound. There is no evidence yet for a commercially appropriate per-customer
  “unlimited” allowance.
- Techmap is called through RapidAPI, at most two fixed-size pages per uncached search,
  with a durable per-owner throttle and short-lived shared cache. **The repository does
  not establish the current Techmap subscription price, included quota or overage cost.**
- Vercel and Supabase are usage-dependent shared costs, not clean per-order variable
  costs. Official public starting prices are currently US$20/month for Vercel Pro (with
  US$20 usage credit) and US$25/month for Supabase Pro (with one Micro project's compute
  credit). The actual ResumeStride account plans and bills were not inspected for this
  research, so no exact application margin should be inferred.

### Stripe economics of a micro-purchase

Using Stripe's public US standard domestic-card rate of 2.9% + US$0.30, before tax,
refund, dispute, foreign-card, currency-conversion, support and infrastructure costs:

| Gross sale | Stripe fee | Net before all other costs | Fee share |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| US$2.99 | US$0.3867 | US$2.6033 | 12.9% |
| US$4.99 | US$0.4447 | US$4.5453 | 8.9% |
| US$19.99 | US$0.8797 | US$19.1103 | 4.4% |

International cards add 1.5 percentage points and currency conversion can add another
1 point under the public standard schedule. At US$2.99, the fixed US$0.30 is the main
problem: the product must generate almost no support burden and must be substantially
incremental.

One domestic US$19.99 Pro sale nets approximately the same processing-before-cost revenue
as **7.34 US$2.99 sales**. If one would-be Pro buyer substitutes a US$2.99 product, about
6.34 additional US$2.99 buyers are needed just to replace the lost net revenue. At
US$4.99 the ratio is about 4.20, so about 3.20 incremental buyers replace one cannibalized
Pro buyer. These are arithmetic comparisons, not conversion forecasts.

**Conclusion:** US$2.99 is technically viable, but commercially fragile. US$4.99 is the
lowest sensible test price for a durable entitlement. Do not use either price for a
single click/download.

Unknowns that must be measured before a margin claim:

- actual domestic/international/payment-method mix, taxes and dispute/refund rate;
- actual ResumeStride Vercel and Supabase plan/usage;
- Techmap plan, quota and marginal search cost;
- production OpenAI p50/p90/p95 input/output use and failure/retry rate;
- support contacts and minutes per purchase/refund;
- conversion and cannibalization by customer intent.

## 3. Four model comparison

Scores are directional: High clarity/value is good; High complexity/cannibalization is bad.

| Dimension | Model A — Current Free + Pro | Model B — Template gating | Model C — US$2.99 Resume Pass | Model D — recommended intent ladder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Customer clarity | High: two choices | Medium: “which design is paid?” can dominate | Medium if “one resume” is precisely defined; low if per-download | High: Build free / Finish & keep / Active search |
| Conversion potential | Misses resume-only willingness to pay | Adds a small Segment A conversion point | Adds Segment A conversion, but low revenue/order | Adds Segment A while preserving high-intent Pro |
| Pro cannibalization | None from a new SKU | Medium if templates were part of Pro appeal | High if it includes broad Match/tailoring or cheap unlimited exports without a boundary | Low–medium because the one-time SKU excludes workflow creation/depth/AI |
| Implementation complexity | None | Medium: template entitlement, locked-state UX, migration | High: ownership must attach to an account/resume and coexist with Pro/refunds | Medium–high: one durable entitlement plus existing expiring Pro; no third workflow plan |
| Support complexity | Low | Medium | High at “which resume/file did I buy?” and repeat-purchase edges | Medium: account-level permanent right is easier than per-resume ownership |
| Stripe economics | Stronger average ticket | Depends on price | Weak at US$2.99 due fixed fee | Better at recommended US$4.99; still requires low support |
| Acquisition value | Current Free and extension remain useful | Risk of making Free look stripped | Good if Free remains useful | Strong: useful Free and extension feed both paid intents |
| Differentiation | Strong Pro story, weak Segment A monetization | Weakens positioning toward template shop | Mixed; “pass” may still look like export toll | Strong: Pro is explicitly active job-search mode |
| Extension fit | Good if capture stays Free | Unrelated | Unrelated | Excellent: free capture → Free evidence → Pro workflow |
| Recommendation | Keep until the new SKU is validated | Do not retroactively gate current templates | Do not launch at US$2.99 | **Recommended after premium inventory and user validation** |

## 4. Exact recommended products

### Free — build and evaluate

Keep:

- the complete resume builder and every current template;
- editable headings, flexible sections, A4/Letter, RTL and worldwide/occupation-neutral use;
- the current three shared PDF/DOCX downloads per fixed 30-day period;
- up to 5 Jobs results, one provider refresh per 24 hours;
- up to 3 saved jobs;
- the concise evidence-based Match preview, without an ATS percentage or hiring claim;
- free extension installation, capture, review and handoff to the signed-in web account;
- access to all owned data and existing job-specific versions after Pro expiry.

Why: this is enough to deliver a real resume and enough workflow evidence to understand
why Pro exists. Reducing the current seven templates to five or removing existing export
utility would trade trust for an unproven micro-SKU.

### One-time — Resume Upgrade, US$4.99 target

Use “Upgrade,” “Unlock” or another permanent-right label; avoid “Pass,” which usually
sounds temporary.

The account permanently owns:

- all premium layouts in the core ResumeStride resume builder, including future layout
  additions within this same template family;
- removal of the ordinary three-per-30-day PDF/DOCX allowance for the account's owned
  resume documents, while retaining a quiet server-side abuse ceiling;
- continued editing and re-export of those documents without another resume charge.

It does **not** include:

- more Jobs results or refreshes;
- more saved jobs;
- full Match depth;
- creation of new job-specific resume versions;
- AI suggestions;
- a separate extension tier;
- application submission/tracking or any future unrelated product.

Why account-level instead of “one resume”: ResumeStride currently has one authoritative
master plus persistent job-specific versions. Binding a purchase to a mutable document
ID creates delete/reset/import/replacement and support edge cases. An account-level resume
entitlement is easier to explain and less likely to feel like repeated nickel-and-diming.

Do not launch this SKU until there are at least three new premium layouts. If users show
little willingness to pay US$4.99, do not solve that by falling to US$2.99; first test
whether the object is valuable and understood.

### Pro — active job search, US$19.99 / 30 days

Keep Pro centered on repeated, opportunity-specific work:

- up to 20 recommendations per search and removal of the Free daily refresh boundary,
  subject to transparent fair-use/provider safeguards;
- expanded saved-job capacity (market a comprehensible number or “more,” not the 10,000
  engineering safety ceiling);
- full Required / Preferred / Nice-to-have evidence analysis;
- creation/reopening of a separate version for a chosen saved job;
- source-grounded AI suggestions with Accept / Reject / manual edit;
- all templates and PDF/DOCX while active;
- the extension-to-saved-job-to-Match-to-tailoring workflow.

This makes US$19.99 obviously different: Resume Upgrade changes presentation/export
rights; Pro changes how the user decides where to invest effort and produces reviewed
job-specific work repeatedly.

Do not promise “unlimited AI.” The current hard global budget and lack of production
usage distribution support “fair-use limits apply,” with a customer-facing allowance to
be selected only after measurements.

## 5. Chrome extension decision

### Recommend: free capture, web entitlements afterward

This matches both the product architecture and the strongest market pattern.

Benefits:

- maximizes Chrome Web Store installs and the top of the acquisition funnel;
- provides real utility before payment, reducing the risk that the listing feels like an
  installer for a paywall;
- makes Segment C an acquisition path rather than a third billing identity;
- keeps entitlement enforcement in the authenticated web/server system rather than in
  extension state;
- keeps the extension's single purpose simple: capture a supported public opportunity
  for explicit review and handoff.

The challenge to the free-extension instinct is abuse/provider cost. ResumeStride's
current design already answers it unusually well: capture is local/session-bounded,
explicit, and does not call Techmap or OpenAI; saving uses normal account ownership and
limits. A free user can therefore capture and use the Free saved-job/Match boundary
without creating an unbounded AI bill.

### Why not Pro-only

- It removes the extension's ability to demonstrate the capture-to-evidence loop.
- It increases installation disappointment and requires the listing/UI to foreground
  that basic functionality is paid.
- Competitors train users to expect basic capture/clipper/autofill utility on Free.
- It adds entitlement/session failure modes to the narrow extension experience without
  reducing the main downstream server gates.

### Why not a separate extension plan

- Segment C's desired outcome is not capture alone; it is capture → Match → version →
  tailoring. That is Pro's workflow.
- A third recurring plan creates plan comparison, upgrade, refund, expiry and support
  complexity while cannibalizing Pro.
- It would encourage ResumeStride to invent extension-only features or allowances just
  to justify the SKU.

### Chrome Web Store policy implications

Google permits free or paid extensions and allows an external payment system. It requires
clear disclosure if payment is needed for basic functionality, honest terms/refund
policies, identification of the seller, accurate data-use disclosure, secure handling,
and a narrow single purpose. Keeping capture free is not a policy requirement, but it is
the simplest fit with the current submitted listing and single-purpose behavior. No
submission field should change while review is pending. If monetization or data behavior
later changes materially, reassess the listing/disclosures before an update.

Official policy sources: [accepting payments](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/accepting-payment),
[quality/single purpose](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/quality-guidelines), and
[user-data disclosure](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/user-data-faq).

## 6. Template strategy

### V1.1 target: 10 total, 7 Free + 3 Premium

Keep all current seven Free. This is both an existing promise and already a useful spread:

- Modern — general-purpose contemporary;
- Classic — traditional;
- Minimal — quiet/minimalist;
- Compact — information density;
- Bold — high visual contrast;
- Executive — restrained senior presentation;
- Ledger — structured/detail-forward.

Three paid layouts are sufficient to test demand **if they solve distinct document
problems**. Count alone is not legitimacy. Recommended new premium briefs:

1. **Technical / Projects-first.** Makes projects, technologies and evidence-rich work
   prominent without assuming a software-only occupation.
2. **Research / Detailed CV.** Handles publications, presentations, research, teaching,
   credentials and longer multi-page histories cleanly.
3. **Portfolio / Design-forward.** Stronger visual hierarchy for creative and client-facing
   work while retaining real selectable text and making no unsupported ATS promise.

Do not build another “minimal,” “executive” or “compact” skin; those jobs are already
covered. Meaningful difference should come from information hierarchy, density,
section behavior, multi-page behavior and occupational fit—not a new font/accent alone.

After the first paid cohort, use selection, preview-to-purchase and refund/support data to
decide whether to add a fourth premium layout. Do not target 30 templates.

## 7. Cannibalization model

### Paths ResumeStride wants

| User path | Desired result | Product response |
|---|---|---|
| Resume-only visitor would never buy Pro | Buys a low-support permanent resume product | Resume Upgrade captures incremental revenue |
| Resume-only visitor needs no premium layout | Completes a useful resume for free | Trust/acquisition, not forced conversion |
| Extension user captures one role | Sees a useful Free Match preview and saves within Free limit | Demonstrates evidence-before-effort |
| Extension/Free user repeatedly evaluates roles | Upgrades to Pro for depth, capacity and job-specific workflow | Primary Pro conversion path |
| Resume Upgrade buyer begins active search later | Buys Pro because workflow value is distinct | Cross-sell; do not create an extension tier |
| Pro expires | Keeps data and can manually edit/open existing versions; AI/new version creation stops | Respectful downgrade and future reactivation path |

### Biggest risk

The largest risk is defining Resume Upgrade too broadly—especially giving it full Match,
new job-specific version creation or low-cost tailoring. That lets an active job seeker
substitute a permanent US$4.99 purchase for the repeated workflow that funds Pro.

The second risk is the opposite: creating premium value by taking away two current Free
templates or making ordinary resume completion frustrating. That may create short-term
micro-sales while weakening acquisition and trust.

### Upgrade treatment

Do not implement a complex universal credit system in the first experiment. At purchase,
show that Resume Upgrade and Pro are distinct and that Pro does not require Resume Upgrade.
Test whether recent Resume Upgrade buyers perceive double charging when offered Pro. If
this appears in interviews/support or materially suppresses conversion, test a bounded
first-Pro credit within 30 days. A credit adds catalog, ledger, refund and edge-case work,
so it should solve measured friction rather than anticipated friction.

## 8. Clean entitlement design

Three concepts are enough:

| Entitlement | Persistence | Allows |
|---|---|---|
| `FREE_RESUME` | Base account state | Builder, current seven templates, Free document allowance, bounded Jobs/Match/saves, free capture |
| `RESUME_UPGRADE` | Permanent account grant | Premium layouts and expanded resume export rights; never AI/workflow depth |
| `PRO_ACTIVE_SEARCH` | Paid-through timestamp | Expanded Jobs/saves, full Match, new job-specific versions, reviewed AI tailoring and all layouts/exports while active |

Expiry rules:

- Expiring Pro never deletes the master resume, saved jobs, analyses or job-specific
  versions.
- Existing job-specific versions remain visible, editable and manually exportable under
  the account's post-expiry export allowance.
- Free users see the Free-shaped Match response after expiry; stored Pro depth does not
  leak through the API.
- Creating another job-specific version or requesting new AI suggestions requires active
  Pro.
- A layout selected during Pro must not corrupt or hide the document at expiry. If the
  account lacks Resume Upgrade, permit editing/preview and provide an explicit choice of
  a Free layout for export; do not silently rewrite the document.
- A purchased `RESUME_UPGRADE` never expires and survives later Pro expiry.
- Account deletion deletes the entitlement with the account; it is not transferable.

One unresolved owner decision is whether buying Pro should permanently grant Resume
Upgrade. Do **not** bundle that by default in the first model: it makes later Pro purchases
from Resume Upgrade owners feel duplicative and creates credit/refund complexity. Pro
should provide all layouts while active; Resume Upgrade is the separate permanent right.
Validate this boundary with users before implementation.

## 9. Landing-page implications (strategy, not final copy)

The pricing model should let visitors self-select by job-to-be-done near the top of the
page:

- **Resume intent:** build a professional document, use the current Free layouts and
  export allowance, optionally purchase a permanent resume upgrade.
- **Active-search intent:** evaluate opportunities against evidence, save the promising
  ones, create separate versions, review tailoring and export.

This is stronger than trying to make every visitor justify US$19.99. It also protects the
brand from becoming a template marketplace: the main narrative remains evidence before
effort; the resume-only route is a clear secondary on-ramp.

The extension belongs inside the active-search story as “bring in a supported opportunity
you found elsewhere,” not as a separate product card.

## 10. What not to do

- Do not charge US$2.99 per download, per click, per format or per job-specific version.
- Do not move any of the seven currently-free templates behind a paywall.
- Do not launch a one-time product before there are distinct premium layouts to buy.
- Do not include full Match, new job-specific versions or AI in the permanent resume SKU.
- Do not sell a separate extension subscription.
- Do not make the extension Pro-only at install/capture time.
- Do not promise unlimited AI or searches before provider-cost distributions are measured.
- Do not advertise the 10,000 saved-job safety cap as an allowance.
- Do not use template count as the primary value proposition or build 30 cosmetic variants.
- Do not silently convert a one-time purchase into renewal or preselect renewal.
- Do not delete, hide or make owned work unreadable when Pro expires.
- Do not alter the pending Chrome Web Store submission during this pricing research.

## 11. Tests before changing pricing

### Qualitative, before implementation

Recruit at least 5–8 people in each primary intent, across occupations and locations:

- Segment A: needs a resume now, no active workflow intent;
- Segment B: actively evaluating/applying to multiple jobs;
- Segment C: finds jobs elsewhere and would start with browser capture.

Test comprehension with a static plan comparison, not a live checkout:

1. Ask which option they would choose and why.
2. Ask exactly what they believe they own after a one-time purchase.
3. Ask what remains after Pro expires.
4. Ask whether US$4.99 feels like a fair permanent upgrade or an export toll.
5. Ask whether a Resume Upgrade buyer expects credit toward Pro.
6. Ask whether free extension capture feels useful before upgrading.

Success criteria before build:

- at least 80% correctly distinguish permanent resume rights from 30-day workflow;
- no repeated belief that US$4.99 is charged per download;
- active job seekers identify full Match/version/tailoring—not templates—as the reason
  for Pro;
- resume-only users can finish on Free without describing the experience as bait-and-switch.

### Commercial experiment, after implementation readiness

Use a staged, time-boxed offer test rather than changing production for everyone at once:

- measure Resume Upgrade purchase rate among resume-completers who hit premium layout or
  export intent;
- measure Pro conversion separately by intent and extension attribution;
- watch the share of Resume Upgrade purchasers who later buy Pro;
- compare Pro conversion against the pre-change baseline;
- track refunds, disputes and support contacts per paid order;
- track paid layout selection, not just purchase;
- track provider and hosting cost per Free and Pro active user using privacy-safe aggregates.

Decision guardrail: do not keep the one-time SKU merely because it makes some sales. Keep
it only if incremental Resume Upgrade net revenue plus later-Pro value exceeds lost Pro
net revenue, incremental support/refund cost and implementation/maintenance burden.
